## 1. Chess with Hidden Information

1a. Describe a variant of chess with hidden information. You should provide
sufficient information that two people could play your variant.

A variation of chess with hidden information as well as a randomized variable could be that the players each take turns pulling cards that correspond to pieces, which they then have to play as strategically as possible as they accept the cards. The hidden information is that neither of the players know what the next card is.

1b. How does your variant change the strategy of chess.

It becomes a much more turn-by-turn kind of strategic game, where big plays and complex plans are harder to achieve due to the randomness. The strategy changes from being focused on your overarching strategy to reacting as best as you can to the card you are given each turn.

## 3. The game loop

Pick one of: Counterstrike, Monopoly, Pokemon the trading card game,
Ultimate Chicken Horse, Starcraft, Mario Kart

Describe its game loop: what the player does, what the game updates in
response, and what feedback the player gets. Then remove one step of the loop
and describe what breaks.

Game: Ultimate Chicken Horse

The game loop of Ultimate Chicken Horse comes down to each round, where the players attempt to get to the flag by themselves. After this is either failed or achieved, the game lets the players pick obstacles to add for the next round. If the game sees that there are too many obstacles or the route is too hard, it gives items to destroy parts of the route. If we remove the parts of the game that allow you to destroy obstacles, the route could very likely and easily become impossible and the game would not progress.

## 4. Ultimate Chicken Horse

Describe one of the placeable objects in Ultimate Chicken Horse that was
especially annoying or frustrating. What was the item and why did you pick it?

For me, one of the most annoying object in Ultimate Chicken Horse was the honey that could be placed on a platform. It interferes with movement, making you slow and affecting your jump. I found it especially annoying because while it can't directly cause a death, it can make a route with already precise movement so much harder with the added changes to your movement along the way. For example, if you don't jump far enough to avoid it, you could jump into it, and it can mess up the rest of your route especially if it's time sensitive with moving and changing obstacles.

## 5. Github Workflow

Forking the repository - Creating your own version of the repository to not affect the original

Cloning the fork - Cloning your fork, so that it's more forgiving if you make a mistake in your own clone. This can especially have a big effect if it's a larger project that's public and has bugs in it, which you would want to have in the clone and not in the fork.

Making changes - Self explanatory; making changes improves the project.

Commit - Making a timeline and saving your changes in the clone

Push - Pushing back to the fork to apply the changes you made in the clone

## 8. Idle / Clicker Game

Come up with an idle / clicker game like Cookie Clicker or Universal Paperclips.

8a. Describe your game.

A clicker game where you control the passage of time, with each click being a second or another small unit of time. You get to observe Earth as time starts to pass faster, until eventually you get to the destruction of earth.

8b. What elements make this style of game fun? How does your game have those
elements?

A lot of people enjoy watching numbers go up with clicker games, and I feel like an ever-increasing rate of time passing would provide exactly that. You would start to see hundreds, thousands, and millions of years go by as you play. It would also have a story element with events that happen to Earth as time passing, like mankind's downfall or the eventual explosion of the Sun.
